# Rail outages

This is one of PayOnce's design notes. [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) has the commands, the file map, the rules that hold everywhere and the index of these notes. Where the two differ, this file is right.

- A rail is `(transfer_currency, transfer_method)` upper-cased, as Airwallex reports them. For PayOnce's own payments that is `(obligations.currency, obligations.transfer_method)`, which every gate reads. An `eur-from-usd` payment runs on EUR SWIFT while its lease is on USD.
- A failure counts when a transfer reads FAILED or CANCELLED with `failure.code` in `RAIL_FAILURE_CODES`. Mixed codes add up, and the outage records the codes it counted (`outages.codes`). Account codes, unknown codes and a transfer with no code never count. Webhook bodies never count.
- `railFailureSeen` is called by the look (after the `bank` line of each attempt its first pass reads as failed, and in `syncOutsidePayouts` for an outside payout that reads failed; the later pass that records a kept transfer again does not call it, and the sweep covers those) and by the sweep for every listed transfer, after it is tied or taken on so the failure names its invoice. Not by the watcher, the listing before a payment or a webhook. It never throws: a failure it could not record is recorded by the next look or sweep.
- A failure is dated by `failed_at`: the transfer's `updated_at` when Airwallex gives one PayOnce can read (when it last changed the transfer, so when it failed, or when it was cancelled a few seconds later), and otherwise when PayOnce first reads it (`seen_at`). It is recorded only if the transfer was created at or after `meta.outages_since` (set on the first open with this code, so an upgraded ledger never counts its old failures). A failure dated by `updated_at` is also left out once that time is a window old, since it could never count. One without `updated_at` is left out once `created_at` plus the method's bank deadline plus the window has passed, so failures read at catch-up after downtime do not count. A `created_at` PayOnce cannot read counts. The window counts `failed_at` (`seen_at` on rows from before the column).
- Two limits. The fallback keeps failures read at catch-up out only while bank deadlines are short, minutes as in the sandbox: with deadlines of days, failures with no `updated_at` read late after downtime are dated when read and can declare an outage together. And PayOnce compares Airwallex's times with its own clock: a host clock more than the window ahead of Airwallex (30 minutes by default) leaves every dated failure out, and one more than the bank deadline plus the window ahead (33 minutes by default for LOCAL, 35 for SWIFT) leaves every undated one out, so no outage is ever declared. A clock behind Airwallex keeps failures counting for longer than the window.
- A payee is `payeeOf`: `keyHash` of the normalised fields that say where money goes (`bank_country_code`, `account_currency`, `account_number`, `account_routing_value1`, `iban`, `swift_code`, spaces dropped, upper-cased), or `beneficiary:<id>` when a payout has none of the account fields. Two beneficiary records for one account are one payee, and a `bank_name` Airwallex rewrote makes no new one. It is not the route key, so outage counts do not move when that key does.
- `Ledger.recordRailFailure`, in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`: `INSERT OR IGNORE` the failure (the transfer id is the key, so FAILED then CANCELLED, or two processes, record it once); if the rail has no active outage, count the distinct payees of the rail's failures dated (`failed_at`, else `seen_at`) after `max(now - window, the rail's last ended_at)`; at the threshold, insert the outage, a `counted` hold for each counted invoice and the declared line on its timeline through `appendEvent`. Failures recorded while an outage is active do not count toward the next one, unless Airwallex dated one after the end by this machine's clock, as when Airwallex's clock is ahead (see the two limits above). The partial unique index `one_active_outage_per_rail` backs this up.
- The gate is `openAttempt`: inside its transaction, after `requireLease`, it throws `RailOutageError` on a rail with an active outage. That covers `startPayment`, `replaceUnderLock`, `useApproval` and `releaseFor`, each of which rolls back, so an approval it would have spent stays `approved`. `Ledger.releaseRun` throws it too when any release row's rail has one. An attempt opened before the declaration is a payment already decided: `sendAttempt` and the retry in `look` are not gated.
- Ledger reads before the lease save Airwallex calls and give the hold its kind: `startPayment` checks first (`original`, so a new original waits open with no attempt and no hold for a person), `continueEscalated` and `continueHeld` after `voidIfStale` (`approved`), and `releaseRun` before `checkRows` (`run`). `replaceUnderLock` reads the outage inside its transaction after `decide` says replace, and `escalateForOutage` writes `Escalate: OUTAGE_REASON`, the `replacement` hold and calls `escalate(..., OUTAGE_REASON)`. If the outage ended in the moment between, the next look is due at once and replaces as usual.
- With `APPROVERS` unset or unreadable, an outage turns every replacement PayOnce would have sent by itself into an escalation nobody can approve: `escalate` opens no request, every approval is refused, and ending the outage does not release it, because an escalated invoice pays only on an approval. Those invoices wait until the list is set. First payments and pay runs held by the outage are not affected this way: they go out after the end.
- No binding depends on an outage. `OUTAGE_REASON` is only `obligations.escalation_reason`, written through `escalate`'s `reason` argument, only for an open invoice whose original failed (never `heldBeforePaying`, so `heldTermsFor` never reads it). The request's terms come from the replace decision through `termsOf`, so `evidence.reason` reads "original failed, a resend can fix it and the beneficiary details are unchanged", and later looks rebuild the same terms. Invoices on other rails, and invoices on the rail escalated for another reason, get identical decisions, terms and bindings.
- `resumeIncident` catches `RailOutageError`: it postpones the due look a minute (`postponeDueLook`) and calls `waitOnOutage`, which writes the hold line once per outage per invoice and the standing `waiting` line, naming a kept approval and when it stops counting. `holdForOutage` writes both in one transaction, only while the outage is active, so neither lands after the end line; the standing line is passed as a `StandingLine` whose `wanted` is `lineWanted`, the check `note` makes. `holdRun` writes a run's `release` line the same way. If the outage has ended, the next look is due at once. `runScenario` does the same for its first send and records the scenario's outcome with `expectOriginal`, so the original sent after the end gets it. No `error` line is written.
- Approvals keep expiring during an outage. One that expires is voided by `voidIfStale` and the next look opens a new request on the same terms, as at any other time.
- A pay run with any approved row on a rail with an outage waits whole, its approval neither spent nor void (`holdRun`: one chained `run` hold line on the run's timeline and a standing `release` line). The watcher tries the release once a minute (`RETRY_RELEASE_MS`), each try a ledger read. Only one run waits at a time, so no other run can be uploaded until this one is released or cancelled.
- Ending: `endOutage(ledger, policy, id, name, note, holdingOf)` checks the name with `personName` and trims the note (1 to 200, `cleanLine`). The name must be in `APPROVERS` (`sameName`, recorded as listed) while the list is usable; with `APPROVERS` unset any named person may, and the end line says no approver list was set. While any approval setting cannot be read (`ApprovalPolicy.unreadable`: `APPROVERS`, `TWO_APPROVERS_ABOVE` or `APPROVAL_TTL_MINUTES`), `outageEnders` gives no names and `endOutage` refuses everyone with the reason (`why` `approver`, 403), so a broken setting never opens it to anyone. `Ledger.endOutage`, in one transaction, first records a hold for each invoice and run `holdingOf` lists (what the board shows as held, read while the outage is still active), then conditionally updates the outage to `ended`, writes the end line on every invoice and run with a hold, `counted` holds included (chained), and sets each held open or escalated invoice's `look_at` to now, so a watcher in any process picks it up. The end line says PayOnce looks at the invoice now only for an open or escalated one; a settled or rejected one is told it is already closed and PayOnce sends nothing for it. Two ends at once end it once; the other gets `ended` (409 with `endedBy` and `endedAt`). The server then wakes the held invoices (an unsent run row is left to the watcher's turn) and starts the held runs' release. The page's end forms and `pnpm outage` show that reason (`endRefusal`, `endRefused` in `GET /api/outages`) in place of the form.
- After the end every check runs again on the next look: a waiting original goes through `startPayment` (duplicates, outside payouts, emails, FX hold, a run row's account and floor), an approval is spent if it still matches or voided for its real reason, an invoice the outage escalated stays escalated and needs its approval.
- The cash panel lists a single incident's original waiting on an outage (open, no attempt, not a run row, an active outage on its rail) as `waiting-outage`. It is in the second headroom figure only, not in `FIRM_KINDS`, so `committedMinor`, the floor reasons and every binding are unchanged. A released run row waiting on an outage is already a `pay-run` line. The incident board shows both as first payments the outage holds (see Outages on the board).
- After upgrading, restart every PayOnce process: an older one ignores outages.

Found by running it on 2026-10-28 (UTC; 2026-10-29 local), on a fresh worktree ledger with `WEBHOOKS=off`, `RESERVE_FLOORS=USD:1000000,EUR:1000000`, `APPROVERS=Alex:10000,Sam:5000,Yash:50000`, the default rule and a loopback mock receiver:

- Two payouts sent outside PayOnce to two USD LOCAL beneficiaries with different accounts, P261028-F9233YT (CHANNEL_TIMEOUT, failed 20:39:28) and P261028-KC0JY2L (SYSTEM_ERROR, failed 20:40:03), were recorded by the sweep 16 and 11 seconds later as one payee each. A `usd-local` incident's original P261028-V7DL95U then failed with CHANNEL_TIMEOUT at about 20:42:25, and the look that read it declared outage 1 on USD LOCAL with codes 91401 and 91402. The same look escalated the replacement with `OUTAGE_REASON` and opened a one-approver request whose terms carried the replace reason. The mock got one "Outage declared on USD LOCAL" post 12 seconds later.
- A new `usd-local` incident started during the outage opened no attempt, got the hold and waiting lines and was listed as `waiting-outage` on the cash panel. Alex's approval of the replacement stayed `approved` and unspent across the one-minute rechecks, and the waiting line named it. An end by a name outside `APPROVERS` was refused with 403.
- `pnpm outage end 1 Alex ...` from the terminal ended it at 20:48:11 while the server ran. Two seconds later the server sent the waiting original (P261028-BDR879Z) and the replacement on the same approval (P261028-XZH7DK4, `approval_id` 2). The sandbox bank paid both and both incidents closed. Pay again was refused by the duplicate lock on each. The mock got one "Outage on USD LOCAL ended" post, which named neither Alex nor the note. Five transfers in all, and `pnpm run audit` verified the chain.
