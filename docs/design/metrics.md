# Metrics

This is one of PayOnce's design notes. [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) has the commands, the file map, the rules that hold everywhere and the index of these notes. Where the two differ, this file is right.

- Metrics are display only. `src/metrics.ts` builds eight tables for a period from the ledger: `7d` and `30d` are 7 or 30 times 24 hours up to now, compared as UTC milliseconds after `Date.parse`, and `all` is every row. Nothing PayOnce decides, alerts on, binds or checks reads a metric, and neither do the board or the cash panel. No Airwallex call, balance or setting goes into one.
- Each table names the time that puts a row in the period. Payments, failures at the bank and fees lost go by the transfer's `bank_created_at`, a refused or never-created payment by `tried_at` (else `opened_at`), and a payout sent outside PayOnce and tied to an invoice by `found_at`. A found payout taken on as an invoice of its own is that invoice's original, so it goes by `bank_created_at` too, which `adoptPayout` writes. Incidents started and duplicates held go by the invoice's first timeline line, incidents closed by its latest `closed` line, approvals by the signoff that completed the request, outages by overlapping the period, and breaks by `opened_at`. A row whose time is missing or unreadable is left out of `7d` and `30d` and counted in the `notOnFile` beside it (`started.notOnFile` for incidents started, each row's own for approvals), while `all` includes it. A duration with either end missing or unreadable, or one that comes out negative, counts in its spread's `notOnFile`. A row whose currency is not three capital letters stays out of the money and counts in `unreadable`. Payments and incidents also give `leftOut`, the invoices a time is missing for, each counted once whether that left it out of the rows or out of one or more durations, and the page's and the CLI's note under those two tables show it. The payments table's `unreadable` counts invoices too.
- The tables. Payments PayOnce sent (an attempt with a transfer, not a found payout's original) by currency and kind (`first`, `first-approved`, `replacement-auto`, `replacement-approved`), with what the bank did to the attempt's own payment (`ownLive`, so a mismatched transfer under it counts only in `mismatchedPaid`, once paid), the first payments paid the first time, the invoices paid more than once since the ledger started (paid attempts and paid outside payouts) and bank time to pay. For those invoices, `paidMoreThanOnce.extra` is what each paid payment after the first took, wallet by wallet: the invoice's attempts in rowid order, a found payout's original included, then payouts in the order they were tied, each at the amount and currency `debitOf` stored, else, for PayOnce's own payment, the invoice amount from the invoice's own wallet or the sell amount of the quote it was sent with when the approval it was opened on (else the invoice's latest approval) names that quote and wallet, else counted in `notKnown`. A found payout's original with no stored amount counts in `notKnown`. Paid mismatched transfers are counted apart in `mismatchedPaid`, at their stored amount or not known, never as a second payment. Failures by rail, stage (`bank`, `refused`, `never-created`) and code, `refused` when the attempt has a `failure_code` or a `refused_at`, so a 4xx with no code counts as refused (one from before `refused_at` with no code still counts as never created), the code shown only as five digits or one of `KNOWN_CODES` (else `other`, and `none` for null), with `FAILURE_WORDS`, whether it counts toward an outage and whether a resend can fix it (`RESENDABLE_FAILURE_CODES`). Incidents started by origin and closed by currency and how (`first-time`, `replaced`, `person-paid`, `not-paid`), how long they took from the first line, from the first failed original and with a person, and the reason on file when a person's invoice closed. An original PayOnce marked failed and then found late counts toward the first failure only once the bank has failed its transfer too, and then with no time on file, because its `failed_at` is PayOnce's mark. Approvals given, needing two approvers, spent, voided and waiting, the time to approve per episode (from the first request after the last one given to the signoff that completed one, since a request renews every `APPROVAL_TTL_MINUTES`), and requests open now. Fees lost, by the cash panel's own `attemptLostFee` and `payoutLostFee`. Invoices held as a likely duplicate, by their state now: stopped (rejected), paid (settled), released (open again, approved and paying) or still held (escalated). Outages with how long they ran, payees, rail codes and their holds other than `counted`. Reconciliation breaks by kind and state, with the time to resolve. A median and p90 are by nearest rank, every median has its n, and p90 is null below 10.
- An invoice needed a person when `escalation_reason` is set. `reasonCode` matches the start of the reason against `REASON_LEADS` (the reason grammar in `why-facts.ts`: the fixed parts longest first, then the parts with slots) and takes the first lead that ends the reason or is followed by `, and `, so a lead that holds `, and ` itself is coded whole. Anything else is `other`. Only the code leaves it.
- Every string in an answer is a code from a fixed set, a five-digit failure code, a currency code, an ISO time with a four-digit year, or a fixed label: `FAILURE_WORDS` or one of three fixed phrases by failure code, `RAIL_FAILURE_CODES` by rail code (looked up with `Object.hasOwn`, so a stored code such as `toString` is dropped), `REASON_LABELS` by reason code, and `KIND_LABELS` by break kind. `metricsRead` selects no supplier name, reference, facts, note, approver or timeline message. In SQL it reads stored JSON only inside `CASE WHEN json_valid(...)`, because SQLite evaluates both sides of an AND and a JSON function throws on text that is not JSON: `duplicate_of`'s length, and of an approval's terms only its quote's sell amount, compared with the attempt's quote and wallet in SQL. Outage codes come back as text, and `outageCodes` parses them inside a try.
- `GET /api/metrics?period=7d|30d|all`, 7d when left out, answers 400 "The period is 7d, 30d or all" for anything else. The server keeps the last three answers, keyed by the period, `Ledger.changeStamp()` and the minute. The stamp is `PRAGMA data_version` and `total_changes()`, so another connection's commit, or the server's own write, builds the answer again.
- The page's Metrics button in the top bar opens `#metrics` (internal id `metrics/`; an invoice called metrics is `#%6Detrics`). It has three period buttons with `aria-pressed` and keeps the choice in memory only. It asks for the metrics when the view opens, when the period changes, and then 30 seconds after the last ask, whether that one came back or failed (`state.metricsAsked`), drops an answer or an error for a period no longer shown, and clears the old period's error when another is picked. It redraws only when the answer changes apart from its times. Tables are built with `h()` and text nodes only. `renderMain` hands the view to `renderMetricsMain`, and otherwise draws the other views through `renderViews` as before and hides the metrics view once another view is showing. At phone width each table scrolls inside its card. At 560px and narrower the top bar's buttons wrap, and the bar, 129px tall at 360px, is static, so it scrolls away with the page. From 561px it is one 56px line and sticks.
- `pnpm metrics [7d|30d|all] [json]` opens `payonce.db` read-only. It refuses a missing file and a file `schemaGaps` finds gaps in, needs no `.env`, and prints each table in the page's order with times in UTC, or the route's answer through `printableJson`.
- The MCP `metrics` tool takes an optional `period` and answers `metricsReport`. Its four label paths are in `PLAIN` and every other string is a token, so nothing in its answer is wrapped.

Checked on 2026-11-03 on ledger copies in the session scratchpad, with no Airwallex call:

- A copy of the real-run ledger seeded with 22 cases (an automatic replacement, a replacement approved after a deadline, duplicates stopped and paid, a request renewed three times, two approvers, an approval voided after signing, paid twice, SWIFT fees kept and not read, a refused create, a never-created attempt, six outages, explained, cleared, pending and unknown breaks, a reopened close, payments exactly at the 7-day edge and a millisecond before it, missing times, a pay run, a payment from another wallet, an unreadable currency, an old reason, a kept transfer and a paid mismatched one): hand-written SQL gave the same figures as `metricsOf` in all three periods at a fixed now.
- On copies of the main ledger and the 5,000-invoice seed, fees lost since the start equalled the cash panel's `fee-lost` lines, started invoices added up to the invoice count, closed ones to settled plus rejected, and every escalation reason had a code (10 and 19 reasons, none `other`).
- The triggers, through the Ledger's own methods inside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` while a second process held read transactions: 33 checks of `escalate`, `rewordEscalation`, `escalateIfOpen`, `holdBeforePaying`, `escalateUnlessSettled`, `reject`, `openAttempt`, `record` (failed, failed again, revived, paid, and the retry that keeps a state to hold the duplicate lock), `failAttempt`, a late transfer, `adoptPayout` and `settle`. Each time was set once, at the change, and never moved, and no statement failed. Main's code on a file this code had upgraded ran the default demo the same and the triggers stamped its attempts. Two processes opening a copy of the main ledger at once added the columns and the five triggers once.
- The default demo through `runScenario` with Airwallex stubbed wrote the same rows as main's code, apart from the new columns. A look on the main ledger's one open approval (INV-362408) under main's code and this code opened the same request with the same binding.
- `metricsReport` on the 5,000-invoice seed took 45 ms warm in one process (42.6 ms of it the read). Through the server, an answer built after another connection's commit took a median of 67.5 ms (at most 140 ms), and a cached one about 15 ms, the same as any small route on this machine.
- With hostile text in every text column the metrics read or sit next to, on a copy of `verify/db/evil`, no seeded string reached the route, `pnpm metrics` text or JSON, the MCP answer or the metrics view's DOM, and the MCP answer held no `outsideText`. The route, the CLI's JSON and the MCP tool gave the same answer. `pnpm metrics` and the MCP tool left the file's hash unchanged. Its `duplicate_of` held valid JSON, so it missed the throw on one that is not JSON (see below). Amounts, fees, counts and the signoffs needed are read as SQLite returns them, and PayOnce writes only integers there.
- The page in headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol: no console error or CSP report on the seeded copy, no horizontal scroll at 360 px with `APPROVERS` set, period buttons switch with no reload, and `#%6Detrics` opens the invoice called metrics.

Checked again on 2026-11-03 after a cold review and a verifier, on ledger copies in the session scratchpad, with no Airwallex call:

- A `duplicate_of` that is not JSON made every period throw "malformed JSON" before. All three answer now.
- Paid more than once on a seeded copy with a paid late transfer, outside payouts with and without an amount (EUR and GBP), a paid mismatched EUR transfer on a USD invoice, and two EUR invoices paid from USD, one whose approval names the extra payment's quote and one whose does not. Before: 1,400,000 EUR over 3 invoices and 160,000 USD over 2. Now: 4 invoices, extra 1,500.00 GBP in 1 payment and 5,377.00 USD in 2, 2 not known, and the mismatched 640.00 EUR on its own line. Hand-written SQL gave the same.
- On a copy of the main ledger, whose 8 closed invoices predate the time columns, the incidents note said 12 before and 8 now, as hand-written SQL does.
- With `/api/metrics` failing, the page asked 26 times in 40 seconds before and twice now, at 0 and 31 seconds. A 30d error arriving after the person switched to all showed against all before, and is dropped now.
- A stubbed 400 with no code on the first send counted as never created before and as refused now. The timeline lines are the same.
- A held duplicate released and paying counted as still held before, and as approved and paying now. An invoice paid the first time whose original was marked failed and then found late had a time from the first failure (200 s) before, and none now.
- At 360px the top bar was 129px and stuck to the top. It is static now and scrolls away. With no `APPROVERS`, the approval card's caution pushed the page to 372px. Every view at 360, 400, 480, 560, 561, 600, 760 and 1280px now has no sideways scroll and no console error.
- 17 stored JSON columns on the board, outage and incident list paths, each made not JSON on its own on a seeded copy: before, 15 of them made all three views throw, approval terms only on an open request, and the run's cash and run approval terms none. Now none does. Through the server, outage codes that are not JSON answered 500 on the three routes before and 200 now. What PayOnce writes reads as before: the three views were byte-identical to main's on copies of the main ledger and a seeded one.
- The verifier's hand-written SQL matched every figure on the seeded, main, 5,000-invoice and real-run copies. `pnpm dev` printed the same 7 decisions as main, and the default demo against a stub wrote the same rows as main apart from the new columns.
