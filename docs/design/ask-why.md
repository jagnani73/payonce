# Ask why

This is one of PayOnce's design notes. [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) has the commands, the file map, the rules that hold everywhere and the index of these notes. Where the two differ, this file is right.

- A person asks why PayOnce did what it did on an invoice, with the "Why this happened" card or `pnpm ask <INVOICE>`. The question is fixed, so nothing typed reaches the prompt. The answer is display-only: never stored in the ledger, the timeline, approval terms, alerts, drafts, `localStorage` or the console, and nothing PayOnce decides reads it. `engine.ts`, `decide.ts`, `approval.ts`, `alerts.ts`, `alert-payloads.ts` and `drafts.ts` never import `why-facts.ts` or `explainer.ts`.
- Every value in the facts is PayOnce's own words, a closed pattern (a bank reference `^[A-Z0-9-]{1,30}$`, a transfer id as a UUID, a status `^[A-Z_]{1,30}$`, a currency, an amount `formatMoney` wrote, a date, an ISO time, a rate `1 EUR = 1.1191 USD`, a count), a failure code in PayOnce's words (`FAILURE_WORDS`, with an Airwallex error code passed only from `KNOWN_CODES`, else "an error code PayOnce does not pass on"), or an alias. A wallet or transfer method that is not a currency code or `LOCAL` or `SWIFT`, as Airwallex can give for a payout found there, goes in PayOnce's words. The invoice explained is "this invoice", other invoices in the ledger are `[invoice N]`, and people are `[person N]` when the name is in `APPROVERS`, signed one of this invoice's approvals, started it, closed it without paying or recorded a callback on it. Any other name is "a person". The legend swaps aliases back only in the answer shown.
- The facts: the invoice (state, origin, run and row, amount, wallet, method, due date, who started it, booleans for a supplier domain and a phone on file, held before paying, the escalation reason, who closed it without paying, the latest decision and waiting lines, the invoices it looks like a second bill for), each payment (kind, reference, state, status, created, deadline and whether it passed, failure, whether a resend could fix it, what it took from the wallet, fee, the approval it was opened on, the approved rate, a rate mismatch as a boolean, found late, a transfer that does not match the invoice, and when it usually arrives), outside payouts, the emails (`emailEvidence` when its text is in the closed form, counts, the three findings, whether the reader failed, and `readBy`, only when it is one of `readerNamesFromEnv` and "the email reader" otherwise), the latest approval picked field by field, `pendingClose` with its reason, the cash (wallet, floor, where the floor comes from, committed money; the balance is not read), the policy as read now, outages (every active one, and every ended one that held this invoice: number, rail, state, times, codes, payees counted, window and what it held), the last 60 timeline lines and how many were left out or not passed on. Never `payTo`, `accountKey`, the beneficiary id, the quote id, the binding, the callback's number, contact or words, the stored findings' `unverifiedSenders`, an email or upload field, `payout_reference`, `supplier_phone`, a run's file name, who ended an outage or its note, or `policy.problem`.
- Reasons (the escalation reason, the reason in terms, the close card's reason and the body of each decision line) go through the grammar: one or more parts joined by ", and ", each matched whole at its position, with backtracking, so a part that holds ", and " is never split. Fixed parts come from the exported constants, and the parts with slots (a currency, an invoice or a list of them, a refusal code, a rate mismatch, a misdirected or late transfer) give only the safe slots. A reason that does not parse becomes "(a reason PayOnce does not pass on)", and the console gets `  why      N unrecognised reasons on <invoice>`, with no text. The arrival clause and the held suffix of a decision line are handled apart.
- Timeline lines pass only through `FIXED_LINES` and `LINE_RULES`, checked in order: a rule keeps a line whose slots are all closed patterns, or rewrites it in fixed words with only the safe slots (sender checks, payouts with their payee or reference, the email delivery line, bank lines with Airwallex's message, waiting lines with `payTo` or the phone, signoffs, callbacks, close notes, outage ends, drafts, a run's first line, the refusals of `send`, the email reader's failure, with a model named only when it is one of `readerNamesFromEnv` and "a model" otherwise, and the sandbox bank's lines, which are `bank` lines, rebuilt from their reference, the status asked for, the HTTP status and a code from `KNOWN_CODES`, and the same lines as `error` lines and the raw simulator errors older code wrote, rebuilt the same way). No rule keeps an error line as written. Each rule and fixed line names the sources it may come from, a kind and a topic such as `decision/decision`, `approval/waiting` or `error`, and passes no line from any other source. An error line holds whatever message the error had, and a callback line starts with a name someone typed, so neither can pass as a decision, a bank line or a closing line. An error line passes only as the fixed words of a rule for one of PayOnce's own errors (a lock wait, a refused or unfinished send, the reader failing, a refused bank change), or as the stand-in. Every line then passes `SAFE_LINE`. A line no rule knows, such as an Airwallex or network error, becomes "(a/an KIND line PayOnce does not pass on)" and is counted in `timelineLeftOut`. A template added later with outside text in it fails closed until it gets a rule.
- The request has the reader's shape (`Authorization: Bearer`, `reasoning_effort: "low"`, a strict `json_schema` of two strings, `explanation` and `notInFacts`, and the 8 second timeout), plus `max_tokens` 4,000, since Gemini counts its reasoning against the cap and gemini-3.1-flash-lite ran out at 1,000 before it answered. The user message is `<facts>` JSON `</facts>`, with `<` and `>` written as `\u003c` and `\u003e`. Each model gets one request, in `READER_MODEL`'s order. A failure (an error status, a timeout, a body that is not JSON, `finish_reason` other than `stop`, other fields than the two, an empty explanation) moves on to the next, and the console gets `  why      MODEL gave no answer after N ms: ...`. A 401 or 403 stops at once. `redactKey` cuts the key out of every error and out of both strings of an answer, since a service that echoes the key in an error could echo it in an answer too.
- `shownAnswer` runs `cleanText`, drops Unicode `Cf` and the tag block, collapses whitespace, caps the explanation at 1,000 characters and `notInFacts` at 300 (at the last sentence end, else the last space with an ellipsis, `cut` true), then swaps `[invoice N]` and `[person N]` back from the legend. `aliasesLeft` says the text still looks like it holds an alias, and only then do the page and the CLI show the legend.
- `POST /api/incidents/:id/why` takes `{}` and goes through `guard`, so another site cannot spend the quota. It does not take the invoice's turn: the facts are read in one snapshot, and the model is asked after it closes. 200 gives `{ explanation, notInFacts, cut, model, at, cached, asOf, legend, aliasesLeft }`. 404 for an invoice not in the ledger, 503 without `READER_API_KEY` (no facts are built), 500 when the facts cannot be built (nothing is sent), 409 while another ask runs, 429 with `retryAfterSeconds` and a `Retry-After` header, 502 when every model failed, saying what each one did in PayOnce's words, which the card shows. `GET /api/options` carries `explainer: { on, models, host, googleFreeTier, perMinute, perDay }`. The server logs `  why      INV answered by MODEL in N s (N characters)`.
- `ExplainLimiter`, per server process and in memory only: one ask at a time; an answer remembered for 10 minutes by `factsKey` (SHA-256 of the invoice, the facts as sent and the model list), at most 50, oldest dropped, which costs no request and counts toward no limit; 2 asks started per rolling minute and 30 per UTC day; and a minute's pause after every model answered 429. The CLI is not limited.
- The card `#why` sits at the top of the incident's side column. It is redrawn only when its state changes, so the poll does not wipe an answer. Off without a key (button disabled, a hint); idle (the hint names the models and Google's free tier when the reader uses Google's endpoint, and the host for any other service); asking (the button disabled with the pulse); answered (the text in `<p>` text nodes, "Not in the records", the legend when aliases are left, the model and time, "Ask again"); cached (says nothing has changed since the time it was asked); stale (a caution when the timeline has a line past `asOf.lastEventId`, shown in place of the cached hint); error (`form-error`, "Try again"); limited (the button disabled until `retryAfterSeconds` has passed, for every invoice; the error that said when goes once the button comes back). Answers are kept per invoice for the page's session only. The page cleans the legend's names and the answer's text the way `pnpm ask` cleans them for the terminal: control characters and line breaks become spaces, and format characters and the rest of the tag block are dropped.
- `pnpm ask <INVOICE> facts` prints the system message, the user message exactly as sent, and the legend, and calls nothing, with or without a key. `pnpm ask <INVOICE>` prints the answer wrapped at 80 and the label. It strips control and format characters from names and invoice numbers after it swaps the labels back, as `pnpm board` does. It exits 1 for a missing argument, no ledger, a ledger an older PayOnce wrote, an invoice not in the ledger, no key (pointing to `facts`) and every model failing, which it says model by model in PayOnce's words. After a request it sets the exit code instead of calling `process.exit`, because exiting while fetch's socket closes trips a libuv assertion on Windows.

Checked on 2026-11-02 on copies of the main ledger, with no Airwallex call:

- Coverage: the facts for every invoice in a copy of the main ledger passed every decision, waiting, cost, bank-details, lock and closed line, kept or rewritten, with no unrecognised reason. Only two raw error lines (a missing fixture file, a simulator 500) became stand-ins.
- Four seeded incidents (a held invoice paid from USD with an open request on a quote, an escalated replacement with an uploaded email and an open two-approver request after a callback, a released pay run row, and a replacement an active outage holds beside an ended one) carried `INJECT-ME-1234` in every free-text field: names, the supplier, the email's sender, subject, body, display name, style text, attachment and Reply-To, the upload, the callback, `payTo`, `accountKey`, the quote id, the run's file name, the outage's ender and note, a close note, a draft's creator, payout references and error text, with phone +1 555 0199 and account 000555997319. A mock chat completions server on 127.0.0.1 recorded every body: none held the marker, the phone, the account or routing digits, or any staff name.
- On the mock: a 503 then a good answer gave the second model; a 401 echoing the key stopped after one request with `[key]` in the error; a hang aborted at 8 seconds per model; not JSON, an extra field and `length` each failed over; both failing gave 502. Bidi and zero-width characters were dropped, `<img onerror>` came back as text, an answer over the caps was cut. The guard gave 403 without Origin or with `text/plain`, and GET gave 404. A second ask on the same facts was cached with one request in all, a concurrent ask got 409, the third in a minute got 429 with `Retry-After`, a line added to the timeline made the next ask a new request, and after 429 from both models the next ask was refused for 60 seconds and then went through. With no key, `explainer.on` was false, the route gave 503, the CLI exited 1 and `facts` still printed.
- Real calls on a copy of the main ledger. The closed automatic replacement (INV-577330, 6,454 characters of facts): `gemini-3.5-flash` explained the channel timeout, the replacement and the close, and put the failure's cause in `notInFacts`. The bank-details escalation (INV-528160): `gemini-3.5-flash` timed out at 8 seconds and `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` answered in 3.3 seconds, `finish_reason` `stop`, 4,267 prompt and 150 completion tokens, 572 characters, with the callback and the two approvers named after the swap. Neither body held a name, the supplier, an address, a phone or an account.

Checked again on 2026-11-02 after a review, on ledger copies, with a loopback mock model and no call to Airwallex:

- Seeded hostile cases. The "only X can approve it" waiting line, whose reason named another invoice and went out raw, now gives `[invoice 1]` and `[person 2]`. An error line that reads like a decision, a bank line or a close became a stand-in, as did a callback whose typed name reads like a close and a decision line with no topic. A reader name made of plain words in the findings became "the email reader". A found payout's wallet and method that were not codes went in PayOnce's words. No user message held the marker, the phone or the account digits.
- On eleven ledger copies (the main ledger and others from earlier runs, 1,425 lines), every line that passed before still passes, with the same text.
- When a service answered 500 with the key cut short, URL-encoded, inside the base64 of its header and as base64url, none of it reached the reader's or the explainer's error.
- `pnpm ask` ran PayOnce's script, while pnpm's own `why` printed nothing and exited 0. The CLI dropped an ESC and a U+202E from a typed starter name, refused a ledger with 52 gaps without changing it, and aliased a starter who is not in `APPROVERS`.
- In headless Chrome the cached hint read "Nothing has changed since 22:33", and a cached answer that had gone stale showed only the stale notice. The minute limit's error went away when the button came back. The hint named 127.0.0.1:4396 for the mock and Google's free tier for Google's endpoint.
