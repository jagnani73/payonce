# PayOnce

Payment incident agent for the Airwallex Agentic Banking Hackathon 2026, built on starter kit 3. TypeScript, pnpm, Node 24. Sandbox only.

## Commands

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm typecheck                                      # tsc --noEmit
pnpm dev                                            # policy on six sample incidents, no credentials
pnpm ui                                             # web page and JSON API at http://127.0.0.1:4310, webhook receiver on 4311
pnpm recover [OUTCOME] [INVOICE] [EMAILS] [SCENARIO] [NAME] # one incident against the sandbox, or continue an existing invoice (status checks, no webhooks)
pnpm approve <INVOICE> [REQUEST NAME]               # show the waiting approval (a replacement, or paying a held invoice), its request number and the policy; with both, record that person's approval of that request
pnpm close <INVOICE> [NAME] [NOTE]                  # show a paid incident or a held invoice waiting on a person; with NAME and NOTE, close it (a held invoice without paying)
pnpm run audit [INVOICE] [json|csv]                 # check every timeline's hash chain and the running hash, or one invoice's; with a format, print its audit trail
pnpm cash                                           # read balances once and print each currency down to the headroom above its floor; exit 1 if Airwallex is unreadable, a floor is unreadable or a headroom is below 0
pnpm reset                                          # move payonce.db aside so the next run starts an empty ledger
```

Defaults: OUTCOME `CHANNEL_TIMEOUT`, EMAILS `nothing-arrived`, SCENARIO `usd-local`, NAME the OS login name (who started the incident). OUTCOME is a simulator failure type, `PAID`, or `STAYS_IN_FLIGHT`, which leaves the original in `SENT`. `pnpm audit` is pnpm's own dependency audit, so the audit command needs `run`. There is no lint script and there are no tests.

## Structure

- `src/decide.ts`: pure decision policy (wait / replace / escalate / close). Rules stay in code; the model never decides amounts or moves money. `floorReason` is its cash check, which the engine also runs before it spends an approval.
- `src/incident.ts`: `Incident` and `Decision` types. Amounts are minor units.
- `src/engine.ts`: the agent, shared by the CLI and the server. `resumeIncident` looks at an invoice: it syncs the payments, reads new emails, decides and acts. `deliver` is the inbox. `startPayment` sends a new invoice's original or holds the invoice for a person. `approvePayment` records an approval of a replacement or of paying a held invoice, `closeIncident` records a person closing a paid incident or closing a held duplicate without paying, `payAgain` puts a person's second payment to the lock, `note` writes the timeline. `Bank` is the hook told about each payment sent. `linkOutsidePayout` ties a payout sent outside PayOnce to the invoice its reference names or nearly matches. The engine never simulates a bank outcome.
- `src/dispatcher.ts`: `Dispatcher` runs steps one invoice at a time. `wake` queues a look at an invoice, and a wake that arrives before a queued look has started is dropped.
- `src/watcher.ts`: the fallback for webhooks. `Watcher` reads every transfer the ledger shows in flight, PayOnce's own and outside payouts tied to its invoices, and wakes the invoice when the bank has paid or failed one. It also wakes an invoice whose next look is due and one with an approval nobody has acted on. Given an interval, it runs the sweep. `lastCheckedAt` is when the last check that read every payment with the bank without an error started. A check with a failed read leaves it as it was, so the panel's "payment states checked" age grows while Airwallex cannot be reached.
- `src/deadlines.ts`: how long the bank has to pay or fail a transfer, by transfer method, counted from the transfer's `created_at`. `isOverdue` treats a time it cannot read as overdue.
- `src/sweep.ts`: `sweep` lists the transfers created since the ledger started and deals with the ones PayOnce did not send.
- `src/duplicates.ts`: `sameInvoiceNumber`, `likelyDuplicates` (the other invoices a new one may be a second bill for), and `nearlyPays` and `invoiceForPayout` (the invoice a payout sent outside PayOnce is for).
- `src/sandbox-bank.ts`: `SandboxBank` plays the bank in the sandbox. It sends a new transfer to `SENT`, pays or fails it when the scenario says so, and pays every replacement. The scripted run sends its original inside `scripting`, and the bank leaves that one to the script. Any other original, such as one released after a hold, gets the outcome `expectOriginal` recorded in this process, or is paid. `PAID_OUTCOME` and `STAYS_IN_FLIGHT` live here.
- `src/scenario.ts`: the demo payments (`SCENARIOS`), `openScenario`, `runScenario` (the scripted incident) and `untilAtRest`. A held invoice stops the script before anything is sent, and no supplier emails arrive for it.
- `src/webhooks.ts`: `WebhookReceiver` and `verifySignature`.
- `src/tunnel.ts`: `openTunnel` starts a cloudflared quick tunnel and reads its address from cloudflared's output.
- `src/assess.ts`: maps an Airwallex transfer to `originalState` and `resendCanFix`, compares bank details, and reads the fee the payer paid (`feeOf`) and what a transfer took from the wallet before the fee (`debitOf`).
- `src/ledger.ts`: SQLite tables for obligations, attempts, approvals, emails, findings and events. `isExpired` and `expiryOf` say when an approval request stops covering a payment.
- `src/payments.ts`: idempotent send and status sync between Airwallex and the ledger. `recordMissingFees` reads the fee of up to five failed transfers the ledger has none for, those never tried or tried longest ago first. A transfer whose fee it could not record is read again only after 10 minutes.
- `src/reserve.ts`: the reserve floors. `reserveFromEnv` reads `RESERVE_FLOORS` (currency:amount pairs, major units), `currencyFloor` gives one currency's floor and where it comes from, `floorFor` gives the floor one invoice's replacement is held to, and `describeFloors` is the start-up line. `debitCurrency` is the wallet a payment leaves, the invoice's own currency for now.
- `src/cash-position.ts`: the cash panel. `cashLines` lists every amount the ledger knows about by kind, `committedMinor` adds up the promised ones the policy and the panel both take off, `payRunLines` is the seam for pay runs and returns null until they exist, and `cashReport` works each currency down to its headroom.
- `src/approval.ts`: `ApprovalTerms` is `ReplacementTerms` (action `replace`, built by `termsFor`) or `HeldPaymentTerms` (action `pay`, built by `heldTermsFor`). `bindingOf` is SHA-256 of the terms JSON.
- `src/bank-change.ts`: `bankChangeForm` says what a person needs to record a callback about new bank details, or why one cannot be recorded now. `recordBankChange` checks the callback, writes a `bank_changes` row, updates the beneficiary in Airwallex and reads it back. It compares with the latest original that became a transfer.
- `src/approvers.ts`: the approval policy. `policyFromEnv` reads `APPROVERS` (name:limit pairs), `TWO_APPROVERS_ABOVE` and `APPROVAL_TTL_MINUTES`. `signoffsNeeded` says how many approvers a payment needs and why, and `checkSigner` says why a person may not approve one.
- `src/emails.ts`: `EmailReader` interface (a `name` shown on the page, and `read`), `EmailFindings`, `summarise`, `unverifiedSenders` and the fixture loader.
- `src/model-reader.ts`: `ModelReader` reads the thread with one chat completion over plain `fetch`, with no SDK.
- `src/reader.ts`: `readerFromEnv` picks `ModelReader` when `READER_API_KEY` is set and the keyword placeholder otherwise.
- `src/keyword-reader.ts`: the fallback reader. It matches a handful of phrases.
- `src/server.ts`: `node:http` server for `web/` and the JSON API. It runs scenarios in the background, starts the watcher and connects webhooks.
- `src/audit-trail.ts`: how a timeline line is hashed (`canonicalEvent`, `eventHash`, `nextLedgerHead`), `chainBreaks` and `ledgerReport` to check them, and `auditExport` and `auditCsv` for the export.
- `src/recover.ts`, `src/approve.ts`, `src/close.ts`, `src/audit.ts`, `src/cash.ts`, `src/reset.ts`, `src/index.ts`: the CLI commands.
- `docs/demo.md`: the recording script for the four demo incidents, with sandbox times measured on 2026-10-06, before the agent waited for webhooks and status checks.
- `src/airwallex/`: `client.ts` (login, token refresh, 30 s timeout, refuses non-sandbox hosts), `transfers.ts`, `beneficiaries.ts` (`getBeneficiary`, `updateBankDetails`), `balances.ts` (`getBalances` and `getAvailableMinor` on the cash account only, and `BalanceCache` for the panel), `webhooks.ts` (`subscribe`), `simulation.ts` (every sandbox-only call).
- `web/`: static page (`index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js`) with no build step and no external requests.
- `fixtures/emails/`: sample supplier threads. `{{invoice}}` is replaced with the invoice number. `new-account.json` uses a look-alike sender domain on purpose. `moved-banks.json` comes from the right domain, says the supplier changed banks and gives no account numbers.

## Rules the engine keeps

- The agent learns what the bank did only by reading Airwallex. A webhook or the watcher wakes it, and it reads the transfer itself. Nothing in a webhook's body feeds a decision.
- Every step on an invoice goes through the `Dispatcher`, including a person's approve and close, so two steps never run on one invoice at once.
- The engine writes an attempt row, with its `request_id`, before the Airwallex call. A 4xx marks the attempt failed. Any other error leaves it pending, because the transfer may exist, and a later look retries it under the same `request_id`.
- Once an invoice is escalated, only a matching approval releases a payment, even if the policy would now replace.
- After sending a replacement the agent waits for the bank. It closes the incident on the look that reads the replacement as paid. A replacement already out is never approved a second time.
- Waiting on the bank has a deadline: the transfer's `created_at` plus `BANK_DEADLINE_LOCAL_MINUTES` (default 3) or `BANK_DEADLINE_SWIFT_MINUTES` (default 5). The look saves it in `obligations.look_at`, and the watcher wakes the invoice when it comes. `startPayment` saves it too, as soon as a new invoice's original is sent, so the look at the deadline comes even when no look runs before it, as when the sandbox bank's script fails. If the send's outcome is unknown, `startPayment` moves the next look a minute on, as a failed look does. An original still in flight then escalates. A replacement still in flight escalates too, with nothing more sent, and the incident still closes if the bank pays it.
- A look that throws leaves the next look as it was. If that time has passed or was never set, it moves a minute on, so a failing look is retried once a minute and not on every check. The watcher wakes an invoice for an approved or expired approval only when no later look is planned. A request that becomes approved makes the invoice's next look due at once, so a watcher in another process uses it on its next check.
- If a replacement has failed, the policy escalates instead of sending a third payment.
- Approvals follow the policy in `src/approvers.ts`, on the page and in `pnpm approve` alike. A name must be in `APPROVERS` and within its limit, compared in the payment's own currency. The person who started the incident (`obligations.started_by`) cannot approve. Two different approvers are needed above `TWO_APPROVERS_ABOVE`, for a payment to an account the original did not go to, and when `started_by` is null. Without `APPROVERS` every approval is refused.
- An approval request expires `APPROVAL_TTL_MINUTES` after it was opened. `escalate` returns the expiry as the next look, and that look opens a new request. `signOff` refuses an expired request inside its transaction, and a request it completes gets at least `APPROVED_USE_WITHIN_MS` (10 minutes) to be used. An approved request that expired before use is voided. A request recorded before expiry existed counts as expired, and the watcher wakes its invoice.
- `escalate` opens no request when `APPROVERS` is unusable or fewer approvers could sign than the payment needs (`eligibleApprovers`). It withdraws any open one and says so on the timeline.
- When one request replaces another that already had signoffs, the timeline says those approvals no longer count.
- An approval is spent with `useApproval` (or `releaseFor` for a held invoice), which only moves an `approved` row that has not expired to `used`, so one another process voided meanwhile, or one that expired during the look, is not spent. Each opens the payment's attempt in the same transaction, after the check that the record still holds the approved account, so the money counts as approved or as being sent at every moment and a failed check leaves the approval unspent. The outside payouts are read again after the last list of transfers.
- The policy escalates a replacement above `TWO_APPROVERS_ABOVE` instead of sending it unprompted. So does an automatic replacement when `likelyDuplicates` finds a match that is paid.
- `ApprovalTerms.accountKey` is a hash of the full bank details the payment goes to, because `payTo` shows only the last four digits.
- New bank details never come from an email or the reader. A person records a callback with `recordBankChange`: the number they called must match `obligations.supplier_phone` by its digits, with or without the country code, and it is only accepted once the invoice is escalated and every payment for it has failed. The details they type are merged into the beneficiary record, sent with `updateBankDetails`, and read back. The change is `applied` only if the record then holds them, with `applied_key` set to the record's `bankDetailsKey`.
- When the record's bank details differ from the original's, `escalate` requests an approval only if `verifiedBankChange` finds an applied change for that beneficiary whose `applied_key` equals the record now. Otherwise it opens nothing, withdraws any open request and waits for a callback. The terms carry the callback in `evidence.bankChange`, the request needs two approvers, and the person who recorded the callback is barred.
- Recording a callback withdraws the open request for the old account. It is refused while another open invoice to the same beneficiary has a pending attempt or an unspent approval, because the change would move that payment's account outside its turn. The fields not on the form must equal the original transfer's, and the call must come after the original was sent. Only the latest applied callback for a beneficiary counts, and everyone who recorded a callback for an account is barred from approving payments to it.
- Every replacement re-reads the beneficiary before it is sent and refuses if `accountKeyOf` the record differs from the approved terms' `accountKey`, or from the details the automatic decision was made on. After sending, a transfer whose details differ escalates with a warning. A pending replacement is first looked up by `request_id`, and is only sent again if the record still holds the approved account.
- Before an approval is used, `approvalProblem` checks it against the policy as it is then: every signoff has the request's binding, there are enough of them, and each signer still passes `checkSigner`.
- A payout PayOnce did not send, whose reference names an invoice in the ledger, is tied to that invoice. So is one that `nearlyPays` an invoice: same supplier, amount and currency, with a reference `sameInvoiceNumber` matches. The sweep ties a near match to an invoice not yet closed first, then to the oldest. A failed payout that only nearly matches a closed invoice is not tied: it becomes an incident of its own, with a warning naming the closed invoice. The check before a payment ties a near match to the invoice being paid, unless the reference names another invoice exactly. While one is in flight, paid or in an unknown status, the policy escalates before any other check, an open approval is withdrawn and nothing is sent. A settled or rejected invoice that gains one is reopened as escalated.
- Before any payment, an original or a replacement, automatic or approved, the agent lists the transfers again and ties any new payout for the invoice. If it finds one, it sends nothing, leaves the approval unspent and looks again at once.
- Any other payout PayOnce did not send becomes an invoice with origin `outside` once it has failed or is in flight past its deadline. The policy escalates where it would replace, so a person approves the replacement.
- No approval is requested for an invoice the ledger shows as paid once.
- Before a new invoice's original is sent, `startPayment` ties any unseen payout for it sent outside PayOnce. It then holds the invoice for a person if `likelyDuplicates` finds another invoice, not `rejected`, to the same supplier (same beneficiary or same name) for the same amount and currency, with a number `sameInvoiceNumber` matches, or if an outside payout for it is live. A held invoice is `escalated` with no attempt and `obligations.duplicate_of` listing the matches. Nothing is sent.
- `sameInvoiceNumber` ignores case, spaces, dashes and other marks, and leading zeros. It also matches a number to the same number with up to four letters added after the last digit, but not two numbers with different letters (`INV-42A` and `INV-42B`, `RENT-2026-OCT` and `RENT-2026-NOV`).
- `heldBeforePaying` is an escalated invoice that has sent nothing: every attempt, if any, failed without a transfer. Airwallex refusing every original (a 4xx on create) escalates the invoice, at once on the first send and otherwise on the next look, so it is held the same way.
- A held invoice pays only on an approval of `HeldPaymentTerms` under the same policy. Each look adds any invoice that `likelyDuplicates` now finds to `duplicate_of`, so one started later, or one that came first while this one was held for another reason, is listed too. The terms list each matched invoice with its state, the payment that paid it, and whether that payment, or one still with the bank, went to the same account. A change in any of them voids the approval. When a matched invoice was paid or sent to another account than the one on file, the request needs two approvers and the timeline warns.
- On a matching approval, `continueHeld` ties unseen payouts and checks the record still holds the approved account. `Ledger.releaseFor` then spends the approval, moves the invoice back to `open` and opens the original's attempt in one transaction, which changes nothing if the approval is no longer approved, the invoice has been closed or has sent something, or an outside payout for it is live. The invoice then carries on like any other. A pending original paid on such an approval is retried only if the record still holds the approved account.
- A person closes a held invoice without paying through `closeIncident`, which calls `Ledger.reject`: state `rejected`, with `rejected_by` set. `pendingClose` offers it as kind `duplicate` when `duplicate_of` lists matches and `unpaid` otherwise. It is refused while an outside payout for the invoice is live. The invoices it matched get a line on their timelines. A rejected invoice that gains a live outside payout is reopened, and when that payout fails, `continueHeld` closes it again without asking anyone to pay it.
- `releaseFor` and `reject` only change an escalated invoice that has sent nothing and has no live outside payout, so a release and a close from different processes cannot both succeed.
- Pay again never reaches Airwallex. `Ledger.tryLock` inserts a replacement attempt inside a transaction and always rolls it back, so the lock either refuses it or nothing is left behind. When the lock does not refuse, the timeline says why nothing was sent.
- A look writes a `bank` line when it finds a payment the ledger had in flight now paid or failed.
- Approval terms take the pay-to account from the beneficiary record as it is now, and the policy escalates if that differs from the account on the original transfer. The timeline carries a warning whenever they differ, whatever reason the policy escalates for.
- A reader failure marks the thread unread, which escalates. The next look reads the thread again.
- A non-finite amount throws in `toMinor`, and the policy escalates if the cash position is not a number. `readMinor` gives null instead, for amounts that are only recorded or shown.
- The reserve floor is per currency, from `RESERVE_FLOORS`. In a currency not listed every automatic replacement escalates ("no reserve floor is set for this currency"). That check comes last, just before the policy would replace, and with no floor set the cash is not checked, so it never takes the place of a more specific reason. A payment sent outside PayOnce is checked before the cash, so the policy's reason for it does not move with the balance or the floor. `CUR:0` means no floor on purpose. A setting that cannot be parsed makes every floor unreadable (`floorFor` returns NaN), so every automatic replacement escalates as "the cash position could not be read". `obligations.invoice_floor_minor` lets an invoice be stricter than its currency, never looser. Of new invoices only `eur-swift-low-reserve` sets one, and invoices from before `RESERVE_FLOORS` keep the floor they carried. An invoice floor above 0 counts as set when the currency is not listed.
- Before an automatic replacement the policy takes off `committedMinor`: money promised to other invoices in the same wallet that has not left `available` yet. That is attempts being sent with no transfer yet, `approved` approvals not used or expired on invoices that could still pay, and approved pay-run rows. Money with the bank is already out of `available`, and requests waiting on a person are not counted, so one held invoice cannot block replacements elsewhere. `assess` adds it up before it reads the balance, so a payment that gets its transfer in between is counted twice, never missed. The panel's headroom takes off the same lines.
- An approved replacement is checked against the floor again before it is spent. `continueEscalated` runs `withFloorReason`, which calls `floorReason`, the check `decide` makes, on the same `floorFor` and `committedMinor` figures, so the two cannot disagree. When the original failed, no outside payout is live and the policy escalated for another reason first (sent outside PayOnce, two approvers, and the like), a floor reason that also applies (below the floor, unreadable or not set) is added to the reason in the approval terms as "<reason>, and <floor reason>", and a standing `floor` warning says so. The decision line keeps the policy's order. An approval of terms without the floor reason is void because the binding changed, and the new request names both. An approval of a request that named the floor reason is spent even below the floor, as in `eur-swift-low-reserve`.
- The cost line on the timeline appears when the replacement pays a fee or something is committed elsewhere, and names the committed amount. The policy's floor reason is unchanged, so no approval is voided by wording.
- Originals, and an original released after a hold, are not checked against the floor. Pay runs will check at run level. The per-currency lock around "read the balance, check the floor, open the attempt" is not built: two automatic replacements in one currency can still both pass if their checks overlap. It belongs with pay runs, wrapping `floorFor` and `committedMinor`.
- Fees are stored on attempts and outside payouts from the transfer's `fee_amount` and `fee_currency`, and are 0 when `fee_paid_by` is `BENEFICIARY`. Decisions still read the fee from the transfer they just fetched, never from the ledger. The panel books a transfer to the wallet it took money from, which for a payout paid from another currency is its `source_currency` with its `source_amount`, and a fee to its own `fee_currency`. No figure adds two currencies together.
- The cash panel caches balances for 30 seconds. The policy never uses that cache: `assess` reads the balance fresh on every look.
- On resume, a paid replacement closes the incident unless an outside payout for the invoice is live. A paid original goes to the policy, which closes it only if no supplier email reports the payment missing.
- An escalated invoice is closed by a person through `closeIncident`, never by a resume, once the bank reports it paid once: by PayOnce's original or replacement, or by an outside payout after every payment PayOnce sent failed. `pendingClose` says when that applies. Closing sends nothing and is not bound to terms.
- A settled invoice that is still paid once is left alone on resume.

## Ledger

- `src/ledger.ts` uses `node:sqlite`, which ships with Node 24, so there is no database dependency or server.
- The file is `payonce.db` in the working directory and is git-ignored. `pnpm reset` renames it with a timestamp, which is also ignored, so the next run starts empty. The rename fails with `EBUSY` while `pnpm ui` has the file open. Transfers already sent in the sandbox stay there.
- The duplicate lock is the partial unique index `one_live_attempt_per_invoice` on `attempts(invoice_id) WHERE state <> 'failed'`. A violation has `errcode` 2067 and is rethrown as `DuplicatePaymentError`.
- `paidOnce` is true when one attempt is paid and every other attempt has failed. `settle` closes an obligation only then. It also voids any approval still open.
- `obligations.escalation_reason` holds the policy's last reason for sending the invoice to a person.
- Approvals live in the `approvals` table with states `requested`, `approved`, `used` and `void`. Each approver's approval is a row in `signoffs` with the binding they approved. `signOff` adds one and marks the request approved once it has `needed` of them, in one transaction, so the server and the CLI cannot both count the last one. `approvals.approver` joins the names for display. The binding is the hash of the terms JSON, so build terms only through `termsFor` to keep the key order stable.
- A new request voids any earlier request or approval for the invoice that is still open. An approval is marked `used` before the payment is tried, so it covers one attempt.
- An approval request is created only when the original has failed, or for a held invoice. `requestApproval` opens one only while the invoice is escalated, in one transaction, so it cannot land on an invoice another process has just closed. `pendingApproval` shows none on an invoice that is not escalated. The duplicate lock still applies to an approved payment.
- Supplier emails and the reader's findings are stored per invoice in `emails` and `email_findings`. The findings record how many emails they cover (`emailsRead`). The thread is read once when new emails arrive and the findings are reused, so a reader that words its summary differently on a second call cannot void an approval.
- `webhook_events` holds the id of every webhook event taken, so a redelivered event is ignored. `attemptOf` finds the attempt a transfer belongs to by `request_id` or transfer id.
- Approval evidence includes the email summary, so new emails that change the findings void an approval.
- `events` holds the timeline. A line with a `topic` says how things stand: the decision, what the invoice is waiting on, an approval, the cost of a replacement, or a bank-details mismatch. `note` skips one when the timeline's last line on that topic says the same and nothing has happened since, so checking an invoice again when nothing has changed adds nothing. A line with no topic records an event and is skipped only when it repeats the line before it.
- On an escalated invoice the decision line says when the policy would now replace or close and why it does not.
- Each timeline line has `prev_hash`, the hash of the invoice's line before it (64 zeros for the first), and `hash`, SHA-256 of `prev_hash` followed by `canonicalEvent`: the JSON `{at, invoice_id, kind, message, topic}` with the keys in that order. `meta.events_head` is the running hash, `sha256(events_head + hash)` for each line in id order, so lines removed from the end of a timeline, or a whole invoice's lines, are caught.
- `addEvent` reads the invoice's last hash, inserts the line and updates the running hash in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction, because the CLI and the server write the same file and a step on one invoice writes on others' timelines. `note` runs its dedupe before calling it, outside the lock.
- `addEvent` hashes the line as it will read back: `storable` turns half of a surrogate pair, such as an emoji cut by `.slice`, into U+FFFD, as SQLite's UTF-8 would.
- The first open with the chain hashes the lines already there in id order and sets `meta.chain_started_at` and `meta.chain_backfilled_through`, the last line it hashed. A ledger whose chain has started (`chainStarted`: any of the three meta keys, or a line with a hash) is never hashed again. A line with no hash, written by older code, is reported as written outside the chain, and the next line chains from the last line with a hash. There is no way to take such a line into the chain, because the same step would launder a forged one.
- `pnpm run audit` opens the file with `new Ledger(path, true)`, read-only, which skips the schema, the upgrade and the backfill. It refuses a missing file and a ledger whose chain has not started.
- `auditSnapshot` reads what a check needs in one transaction. The check compares each line with the stored hash of the line before it and with its own content, so an edit shows up once. When the running hash does not match but every timeline verifies, it cannot say which invoice lost lines.
- `meta` holds `started_at`, when the ledger was first opened. The sweep lists transfers from then, so payouts from an earlier ledger are not taken for outside ones.
- `obligations.supplier_phone` is the phone number on the supplier record, set from the scenario. Only `usd-local-bank-change` has one, so only it offers a callback. Found payouts have none.
- `bank_changes` holds each callback: who called, the number, when, who they spoke to, what was confirmed, the old record and the typed fields, and `state` `pending`, `applied` or `failed`. The row is written before the Airwallex update.
- `obligations.state` is `open`, `escalated`, `settled` or `rejected` (closed without paying). The watcher, `dueLooks` and the server's start-up pick-up skip `settled` and `rejected` invoices.
- `obligations.duplicate_of` is a JSON list of the invoices a held invoice looked like, and `rejected_by` is who closed it without paying.
- `obligations.origin` is `payonce` or `outside`. `payout_reference` keeps a found payout's reference when it could not be the invoice number, and `obligationForReference` matches either.
- `obligations.invoice_floor_minor` is an invoice's own floor, 0 for none. `reserve_floor_minor` is the floor each invoice carried before `RESERVE_FLOORS`. The open that adds `invoice_floor_minor` copies every `reserve_floor_minor` that is not 0 into it, and only that open, so no invoice from before is held to a looser floor than it was. After that `reserve_floor_minor` is not read or written.
- `attempts.fee_minor` and `fee_currency` hold the fee as last read from the transfer. `record` keeps the stored fee when a read has none. `recordFee` writes only these columns, and only where `fee_minor` is null, so it can run outside an invoice's turn. `failedWithoutFee` lists failed transfers, PayOnce's and outside payouts, with no fee recorded, leaving out those tried too recently and putting the least recently tried first. `fee_tried_at` on both tables is when `feeTried` last marked a transfer before its fee was read. `latestFee` is the latest fee recorded in a currency by a transfer method, which the panel uses to estimate the fee of a payment not sent yet.
- `outside_payouts.amount_minor`, `currency`, `fee_minor` and `fee_currency` are written when a payout is tied and on every look that reads it. `attempts.amount_minor` and `currency` are written by `record` and `adoptPayout`. On both tables the amount and currency are what the transfer took from the wallet before the fee (`debitOf`): the source amount and currency when it was paid from another currency, and the transfer amount otherwise. A row from before has none, and the panel then takes the invoice's amount in the invoice's currency and marks it estimated.
- `adoptPayout` writes a found payout's invoice and attempt in one transaction. Its INSERT is plain, so an invoice written by another process in between makes it fail instead of gaining a second original.
- `outside_payouts` ties outside payouts to invoices, with the bank's short reference and the last state a look read. `settle(invoiceId, true)` also accepts an invoice whose attempts all failed and whose outside payout is paid. Only `closeIncident` passes it.
- Columns added after the first version are listed in `ADDED_COLUMNS` and added when the database is opened, under the write lock, so two processes opening an older file at once do not both add one.

## Email reader

- `READER_API_KEY` in `.env` turns the model reader on. `READER_BASE_URL` defaults to Gemini's OpenAI-compatible endpoint. `READER_MODEL` is a comma-separated list tried in order and defaults to `gemini-3.5-flash,gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, both on Google's free tier.
- `pnpm dev` loads no env file, so it always uses the keyword placeholder.
- The request is one chat completion with `reasoning_effort: "low"` and a `json_schema` response format. The model answers two booleans and no text. `toAnswers` checks the answer again, so a service that ignores the schema still cannot return anything else.
- `summarise` in `src/emails.ts` writes the summary from the two findings for both readers, so no wording from an email or a model reaches the approval card or the approval binding.
- `ModelReader` also runs the keyword check and ORs the findings. Both findings only make the policy more cautious, so a tricked model cannot remove a finding the keywords make. It can still hide one the keywords miss.
- Each model gets one request with an 8 second timeout. If it fails, the next model is asked, and the console gets a `reader` line saying why. A refused key (401 or 403) stops at once. If every model fails, `read` throws, the engine marks the thread unread, and the policy escalates.
- Findings carry `readBy`, the model that answered, and the page shows it.
- The API key is cut out of any error text before it reaches the timeline.
- Google may use free-tier content to improve its products. The fixtures are synthetic.

Found by running it on 2026-10-06:

- The key goes in `Authorization: Bearer`. A strict `json_schema` response format and `reasoning_effort: "low"` are accepted, and a good answer has `finish_reason` `stop`.
- `gemini-3.8-flash`, the model in Google's examples, answered 503 "high demand" on 5 of 8 calls. In about 35 reader calls, `gemini-3.5-flash` and `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` between them always returned an answer, usually in 1.6 to 5.8 seconds. `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` took 8 to 18 seconds. The 2.5 models return 404 for new users.
- `gemini-3.5-flash` returns 429 after about six calls in a minute and answers again a minute later. The second model answered each time. Daily limits are shown only in AI Studio.
- Both default models gave the expected findings on the two fixtures, a reworded email the keywords miss, a thank-you email and an email with an injected instruction to answer false. The one difference: `gemini-3.5-flash` once read the injection email as a non-receipt claim.
- One "new account" incident on the page took 21 seconds, against 8 with the keyword reader. Its first read took 12 seconds, before the timeout was cut from 15 seconds to 8.

## Web server

- It binds to loopback and has no login. `guard` checks the Host header and, for writes, the Origin header and a JSON content type.
- Routes: `GET /api/options`, `GET /api/cash`, `GET /api/incidents`, `GET /api/incidents/:id`, `GET /api/incidents/:id/audit` (JSON, or CSV with `?format=csv`), `POST /api/incidents` (takes `bankOutcome`, `emails`, `scenario`, `startedBy` and an optional `invoiceId`, refused with 409 when it is already in the ledger), `POST /api/incidents/:id/approve` (needs `approver` and `approvalId`), `POST /api/incidents/:id/close` (needs `closedBy` and `finding`), `POST /api/incidents/:id/resume`, `POST /api/incidents/:id/pay-again`, `GET /api/incidents/:id/bank-details`, `POST /api/incidents/:id/bank-change` (takes `calledBy`, `numberCalled`, `calledAt`, `spokeTo`, `confirmed` and `details`).
- An incident's detail carries `audit`: the number of lines, and `problem`, the first break in the chain or a running hash that is missing or does not match, null when there is none. The page shows it under the timeline with links to the export. It carries `review` when a person can close it, and the page shows the close card from that. `busy` is true while the agent or the sandbox bank is working on it, and `watching` while a payment is with the bank, including an outside payout tied to it. The page polls every 1.5 seconds while either is true.
- Approve and close wait for any step already running on the invoice, then run. Resume queues a look.
- `GET /api/cash` answers at once from `BalanceCache` and the ledger. It starts one balance read when the last try, good or failed, is 30 seconds old, so a page that stays open reads the balance at most twice a minute and a closed one reads nothing. The server reads once at start. After each good read it runs `recordMissingFees`, so while failed transfers have no fee one refresh makes up to six reads: the balance and five transfers. Because it answers before the read it starts, the first answer after nobody asked for a while carries a read older than 30 seconds, with `refreshing` true. The page then says it is reading the balances again and polls every 1.5 seconds until the read lands. A failed read keeps the last good one, and the answer carries the error, the read's age and `statesCheckedAt` from the watcher. Headroom is null when the balance was never read, Airwallex has no balance in that currency, or `RESERVE_FLOORS` is unreadable. `payRuns` is null until pay runs exist.
- A currency is listed when it has a floor set, a line in the ledger, an invoice floor or a balance that is not zero. The page shows the cash panel above the incident, one row per currency. A currency is open by default when money in it is promised or waiting on a person, or a headroom is below 0. A person's click is kept in `localStorage` under `payonce.cashOpen`. The panel is redrawn only when the figures change, and the ages are set on their own nodes on every poll.
- The page scrolls to keep the newest timeline line on screen when that line was already visible, and for 15 seconds after an action.
- On start it wakes every invoice that still has a pending or in-flight attempt, and the sandbox bank goes back to paying any replacement still in flight, and an original in flight that was paid on an approval after a hold.
- The watcher sweeps every 30 seconds. A paid, failed, cancelled or overdue webhook for a transfer the ledger does not know asks for a sweep on the next check. One for an outside payout already tied to an invoice wakes that invoice.
- `GET /api/options` lists the default email thread first, so the form opens on the incident that is replaced without a person. It also lists the approvers and the approval policy, and an incident's approval carries its signoffs, `needed`, `expiresAt` and who is barred, so the page can offer only the names that may approve.

## Webhooks

- `pnpm ui` starts `WebhookReceiver` on `WEBHOOK_PORT` (4311, loopback). It answers `POST /airwallex/webhooks` and nothing else, so the tunnel exposes no other route. It answers 503 until it has the secret, so Airwallex retries.
- Without `WEBHOOK_URL`, `openTunnel` runs `cloudflared tunnel --no-autoupdate --url http://127.0.0.1:4311` (`CLOUDFLARED` sets the path) and takes the `trycloudflare.com` address from its output. The address changes on every start.
- `subscribe` finds PayOnce's subscription by the `request_id` prefix `payonce-transfers-` and moves it to the new address, or creates it with all nine `payout.transfer.*` events. The secret comes back from Airwallex and is held in memory only.
- `verifySignature`: HMAC-SHA256 hex of the `x-timestamp` header followed by the raw body, keyed with the secret, compared in constant time. A timestamp more than 5 minutes off is refused.
- The receiver takes the transfer from `data`, or from `data.object` when Airwallex nests it there.
- Paid, failed, cancelled and overdue events for PayOnce's own transfers wake the agent. They put a `bank` line on the timeline unless the ledger already shows the payment paid or failed. Other events are only logged.
- With webhooks connected the watcher checks every 15 seconds. Without them it checks every 3, and the CLI every 2. `WEBHOOKS=off` skips the receiver, the tunnel and the subscription.
- `WEBHOOK_API_VERSION` defaults to `2025-06-30`. No response header says which version the account uses.

Found by running it on 2026-10-28:

- A webhook `request_id` is limited to 50 characters.
- The list call returns each subscription's secret. The update call moved PayOnce's subscription to a new tunnel address and kept its id.
- In three incidents, with about ten transfer status changes, Airwallex delivered no event within 12 minutes. The tunnel answered a request from outside, and the subscription listed the right address and events. The watcher caught every change. Delivery status per event is under Settings > Developer > Events in the web app, which needs the account owner's login.
- Checked in the web app later the same day: Airwallex creates an event for every status change (`scheduled`, `processing`, `sent`, `paid`, `failed`), with the transfer directly under `data` and `version` `2025-06-30`, addressed to the subscription's URL. Every event stayed `Queued` with no HTTP status, so none was ever attempted. With a fresh tunnel and the subscription moved to it, "Send test event" and "Re-trigger" in the web app delivered nothing either, while a POST from outside through the tunnel reached the receiver and got its 401. The sandbox records events but does not deliver them to this account.
- The scripted incidents with the watcher on: a replaced payment from start to close in 11 seconds through `pnpm recover`, and about 50 seconds for each escalation on the page while the watcher checked every 60 seconds.
- `winget install Cloudflare.cloudflared` waits on a UAC prompt. The standalone `cloudflared-windows-amd64.exe` from the GitHub release runs without admin rights.

## Sandbox behaviour found by running it

- Credentials are a scoped API key in `.env` (`AWX_CLIENT_ID`, `AWX_API_KEY`). Admin keys are deprecated.
- A new transfer starts `SCHEDULED` and moves to `PROCESSING` on its own within seconds. The simulator returns `500 operation_failed` for a transition requested while it is still `SCHEDULED`, so wait first.
- The simulator sometimes returns `500 operation_failed` after it has applied the transition. `simulateTransfer` reads the transfer after a server error and retries only if the target status was not reached.
- A transfer left in `SENT` was `PAID` about ten minutes later with no simulation call. This happened once.
- `PROCESSING` to `SENT`, `SENT` to `PAID` and `SENT` to `FAILED` (with `failure_type`) all work. A failed transfer reads `FAILED`, then `CANCELLED` a few seconds later. The `failure` object survives the change.
- `failure.details.type` is `INCORRECT_ROUTING` for every simulated failure. Read `failure.code` instead: 91401 system error, 91402 channel timeout, 90701 account closed, 90101 invalid account name or number, 90802 beneficiary bank returned, 91001 recall requested, 91301 duplication return, 99901 unable to apply, 99902 other.
- Creating a transfer takes the amount out of `available_amount` immediately. A failed transfer is refunded a few seconds later.
- A EUR SWIFT transfer of 4,000 carried a fee of 13.91 or 13.92 EUR. When it failed, the 4,000 came back and the fee did not.
- The beneficiary record and a transfer's `beneficiary.bank_details` hold the same object. The transfer keeps the details it was sent with: `POST /api/v1/beneficiaries/{id}/update` changes the record and not the transfer, and setting the old values back makes the two equal again.
- Reusing a `request_id` returns `400 duplicate_request_id` and names the original transfer.
- Run on 2026-10-28: `POST /api/v1/beneficiaries/{id}/update` with the GET record's `beneficiary` (without `fingerprint`), `nickname`, `payer_entity_type` and `transfer_methods` was accepted. Airwallex rewrote `bank_name` from the routing number ("Bank of America" became "Bank of America, NA"), so the read-back compares only the fields that say where money goes. The next transfer to the beneficiary went to the new account.
- The scenarios share one beneficiary per currency, so a bank change on one would reach the others. Each `usd-local-bank-change` run creates a beneficiary of its own, nicknamed `PayOnce demo supplier (bank change)` plus the invoice number.
- LOCAL USD transfers carry no fee in the sandbox.
- Run on 2026-10-28: `reserved_amount` and `pending_amount` in `GET /api/v1/balances/current` read 0 in USD while a 4,000 USD transfer was in `SENT`. Money in flight cannot be read from the balance, so the panel takes it from the ledger.
- The client sends no `x-api-version` header. The account default returns the newer status set (`FAILED` exists) with a nested `failure` object.
- Airwallex emails the account owner for every sent and cancelled transfer unless transfer notifications are turned off under Settings > User settings > Notifications. On 2026-10-28 the email for cancelled transfers was turned off there. Sent, overdue reminders and the daily summary of scheduled transfers were already off.
- `GET /api/v1/transfers` takes `from_created_at` as an ISO time with milliseconds and `Z`, and takes `status`. It lists newest first and pages with `page_size` and a `page` cursor taken from `page_after`.
- A transfer's `beneficiary` has `bank_details`, `address`, `entity_type` and `type`. `bank_details.account_name` is the only name on it.
- `created_at` reads like `2026-10-27T19:48:09+0000`, which `Date.parse` accepts.
- Runs on 2026-10-28. With the local deadline set to 1 minute, an original left in `SENT` escalated 63 seconds after it was sent. A failed payout made outside PayOnce was taken on within 30 seconds, and its approved replacement was paid and the incident closed 6 seconds after the approval. An outside payout made just before an approval was tied by the check before payment one second after the approval: the approval was voided and nothing was sent. When the bank paid that payout, the close card offered it.
